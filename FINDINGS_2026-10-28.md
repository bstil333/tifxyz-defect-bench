# How well do the community tifxyz checkers find planted defects?

A measurement of tifxyz-doctor, tifxyz-repair and windcheck against a labeled benchmark.

**Run:** 2026-10-28 · 2 public segments × 10 seeds × 4 severity levels = 80 cases · 3 tools · 240 tool-case runs, 0 errors · about 25 seconds in total · all figures are produced by `summarize.py` from `results.json`.

## Summary

The tool READMEs say no labeled corpus exists to measure how often these checkers find real problems. This benchmark plants defects with known positions in clean segments and asks each tool whether it flags them.

1. **Holes, normal flips and stretches are found reliably by at least one tool with almost no false alarms.** tifxyz-repair's hole cue found 240 of 240 planted holes with 0.00 flags per 1,000 cells on clean surfaces. tifxyz-doctor found 100% of normal flips and stretches (240 of 240 each) with 0.37 flags per 1,000 cells.
2. **Sheet jumps are the weak spot for every tool.** A sheet jump is a block displaced along its normal. tifxyz-doctor found none at 5 or 10 voxels (0 of 120), 3 of 60 at 20, and 31 of 60 at 40. windcheck found 15% at 5 voxels and 68% at 40. Combining tifxyz-doctor and windcheck reached 83% (50 of 60) at 40 voxels, and 15% at 5.
3. **tifxyz-repair's headline recall is not evidence of detection.** Its "self_proximity" cue flags 8.3% of valid cells on clean surfaces (83.13 per 1,000), so a flag lands near almost any defect by luck. Its other two cues are quiet on clean data and are the informative ones.
4. **windcheck had zero false alarms** and finds stretches (95%) and about half of sheet jumps (52%). It is a self-intersection detector, so it is not expected to find holes (3%) or normal flips (25%).

## What was measured

- **Clean surfaces.** Two public PHerc0332 segments (grids 126 × 1286 and 138 × 1266; 267,342 valid cells together).
- **Planted defects**, 3 of each kind per case, in non-overlapping blocks of 10 × 24 cells with exact positions:

| kind | what is done to the block |
|---|---|
| hole | every cell set invalid |
| sheet_jump | displaced along its local normal by 5, 10, 20 or 40 voxels |
| normal_flip | grid order reversed (the parameterization folds at its edge) |
| stretch | scaled about its own center by 1.1, 1.25, 1.5 or 2.0 |

- **Found** means any cell the tool flagged lies within 2 cells of the planted block.
- **False alarms** are cells flagged on the clean segment, per 1,000 valid cells.
- **Chance baseline.** A tool that flags many cells everywhere would "find" defects by luck. For each tool the report gives the probability that a defect-sized neighborhood (392 cells) contains at least one flag if the tool's clean-surface flags were scattered at random. Recall near that number is not evidence of detection.
- Every tool ran at its **default settings** and nothing was tuned. The code that turns each tool's output into flagged cells never reads the answer key.

## What this suggests

- **For holes, flips and stretches** the tools are in good shape. The practical choice is which false-alarm rate you can live with, and the specific cues (repair's hole and spacing cues, doctor) are all very quiet on clean data.
- **For sheet switches, the failure users care about most, small displacements are mostly missed.** At 20 voxels or less, tifxyz-doctor found 3 of 180. windcheck finds more, but it detects self-intersection, which a pure shift along the normal produces only some of the time. The two tools complement each other, but even together they found only 9 of 60 at 5 voxels.
- **tifxyz-repair's self_proximity cue needs a different threshold or a different baseline on spiral surfaces.** Neighboring wraps of a rolled sheet legitimately sit close in 3D, and on these two clean segments the cue flagged 8.3% of cells. That is a finding about the cue's default on real geometry, not about the tool as a whole.
- **Stretch as implemented here is a discontinuity at the block edge,** so all three tools find most of them even at 1.1× (85% to 100%). It says nothing about smooth, gradual stretch.

## Limits

- **Two segments only.** The seeds change where defects sit, not the surface, and defects in one segment are not independent, so the intervals above are optimistic.
- **Synthetic defects on clean surfaces.** Nothing here says how the tools behave on real autosegmentation errors, which are messier.
- **Generous hit rule.** Any flag within 2 cells counts, which favors tools that flag more. That is why the chance baseline is shown.
- **tifxyz-doctor's JSON lists at most 50 located examples per finding type,** so its recall here may be lower than what its HTML or overlay output shows.
- **windcheck is a self-intersection detector;** its low hole and flip recall is outside its stated purpose.
- The adapters that turn each tool's output into flagged cells were written with a local coding assistant from specifications, then reviewed by hand. They use default settings and cannot read the answer key.
- The tool authors have not reviewed this report.

## What would make the next version stronger

1. Real defective segments (autosegmentation output with human-marked errors) alongside the synthetic ones.
2. More source segments, so the intervals reflect surfaces rather than placements.
3. A smooth stretch and a graded hole size, so each cue is tested across its range.
4. A per-cue report for every tool, as done here for tifxyz-repair, so a threshold choice is made cue by cue.
5. The authors' own review of how their tools were invoked.

## Reproduce

All code is MIT-licensed in `tifxyz-defect-bench/`. From `vesuvius_qa/`: set up one virtual environment per tool (`venvs/<tool>`, each installed from its public repository), then

```
python tifxyz-defect-bench/run_bench.py --seeds 10
python tifxyz-defect-bench/summarize.py runs/<run directory>
```

`results.json` holds every case and every tool result, including errors.
